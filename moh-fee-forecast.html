Three Non-Obvious Findings
The Flat Fee Alone Raises More Than Most Assume
Volume effect
Simply raising RM1 to RM10 for everyone generates RM571.5M/year in new revenue because outpatient volume is enormous.
Malaysia Has Normalised the Habit
Infrastructure precedent
BUDI95 proves MyKad eligibility checks can happen at national scale. The missing piece is a product-category registry queryable by hospitals.
30% of Adults May Understate Visit Share
Hidden assumption
The model assumes equal hospital utilisation. Smokers, vapers, and drinkers may have higher NCD-related utilisation in reality.
The Revenue Number Is Not the Main Benefit
Compounding system relief
The long-run upside is the combination of revenue, fewer low-acuity walk-ins, lower future NCD burden, and a plausible welfare contribution to doctor retention.

Executive Summary

This brief models two hypothetical reforms to Malaysia's RM1 government hospital outpatient fee: a flat increase to RM10 for all citizens, and a tiered system charging RM30 to individuals flagged via IC verification as buyers of cigarettes, vape, or alcohol, with RM10 for everyone else.

Using 2023 MOH outpatient volume of 63.5 million visits and national prevalence data, the flat increase alone generates an estimated RM571.5 million/year in new revenue, while the tiered model generates approximately RM1.02 billion/year.

The model's core implementation challenge is not the pricing math. It is the creation of a product-specific purchase registry, privacy-safe cross-system querying, and a legally credible ring-fenced Frontliner Welfare Fund.

Recommended Sequencing
  1. 01
    Separate the two reforms
    Implement the administratively simple RM1 to RM10 flat increase first while designing the harder tiered registry properly.
  2. 02
    Pilot IC verification in one state
    Test retail-to-registry-to-hospital data flow, accuracy, administrative burden, and privacy controls before scaling.
  3. 03
    Legislate the Frontliner Welfare Fund
    Ring-fencing needs statutory credibility before public expectations are set.
  4. 04
    Build income adjustments and exemptions
    Avoid a uniform surcharge that penalises B40 patients more severely.
Conclusion

The revenue math is compelling, but the tiered system is ultimately a national digital infrastructure project wrapped in healthcare policy. It is worth feasibility study because it could pull three levers at once: new frontliner welfare revenue, reduced low-acuity demand, and a possible contribution to doctor retention. It should not be treated as a quick pricing tweak.
